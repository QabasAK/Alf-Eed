# Pick your number

Propose three numbers for the team's field. A good one meets all of these:

1. **It's true.** The story behind it is real and you can name a source. Never make one up.
   If you aren't confident in an anecdote, say so or drop it.
2. **People in the field would grin.** It should reward insiders, not need a paragraph of
   explaining. Favour a beloved mistake or an obsession over a textbook constant.
3. **It's whole and achievable.** Each term takes a definition, a filmed sign and a video,
   and nobody can film part of a sign. Stay at or below about 60 terms. If the best joke is
   a bigger number, find a smaller true one, and let the joke admit what you left out.
4. **It's kind.** The joke is about the field, never about Deaf people or disability.
5. **It's not taken.** Check it against the wall below.

## The wall so far

| Field | Number | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering (including computer engineering) | 42 | The answer to life, the universe and everything |
| Mathematics | 57 | Grothendieck's "prime". It's 3 × 19 |
| Physics | 17 | One per particle in the Standard Model |
| Chemistry | 18 | One per column of the periodic table |
| Medicine | 33 | One per vertebra |
| Literature | 28 | One per letter of the Arabic alphabet |
| Architecture | 10 | Vitruvius's ten books on architecture |

## Output

For each candidate, give the number, the story in two sentences, a source, a suggested split
into categories, and a rough workload. Then recommend one and write a one-line joke for the
back of its card, in English and Arabic.

A number that doesn't divide evenly isn't disqualifying. Say how you'd split it anyway.
